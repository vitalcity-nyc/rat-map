    How we did this

    Sources

    All data come from NYC Open Data, accessed {{ACCESSED_LONG}}: 311 Service Requests from 2010 to 2019 ({{N_OLD}} rat sightings) and 311 Service Requests from 2020 to Present ({{N_NEW}} rat sightings through {{LAST_DATA}}), filtered to complaint type "Rodent" with descriptor "Rat Sighting"; the DOHMH Rodent Inspection dataset ({{N_INSP}} initial inspections); Community District and 2020 Neighborhood Tabulation Area boundaries (shoreline-clipped); and the Department of City Planning's 2020 decennial census tables for populations.

    Calculations

    The block-level map aggregates sighting coordinates to a rectangular grid of roughly 220 meters on a side (0.002° latitude by 0.0026° longitude); {{PCT_NOCOORD}} of sightings lack usable coordinates and are excluded from maps but included in citywide counts. Year-over-year change compares {{YTD_LONG}} windows so the partial year {{CUR}} is compared fairly against the same months of {{PREV}}. Rates divide sighting counts by 2020 census populations (held constant across years) and are shown per 10,000 residents for neighborhoods and districts, per 100,000 for boroughs. "Inspection failures" is the share of the health department's initial rodent inspections — excluding follow-up compliance visits and treatments — whose result was "Failed for Rat Activity" or "Failed for Rat Activity and Other Reason." Inspections with impossible dates (before 2010 or in the future; {{N_BADDATE}} records, {{PCT_BADDATE}}) and inspections without usable coordinates ({{N_INSPNOCOORD}}, {{PCT_INSPNOCOORD}}, for map views only) are excluded. The borough trend view uses 12-month rolling averages to smooth seasonal swings. The hour-of-day chart excludes {{N_MIDNIGHT}} records ({{PCT_MIDNIGHT}}) stamped exactly midnight, which are date-only entries. The address lookup counts sightings within a straight-line radius of the geocoded point, using locations rounded to about 28 metres.

    Limitations

    311 sightings measure reporting, not rats: neighborhoods differ in their propensity to call, and campaigns or news coverage can move the numbers independent of the rodent population. That is why the inspection failure rate is shown alongside them — it is drawn from inspector observations rather than complaints, though inspections themselves are partly complaint-driven, so the two measures are not fully independent. The community-district assignment uses the district recorded by 311, which is missing or unspecified for {{PCT_NOCB}} of records overall, concentrated in 2014–2018 (up to a few percent of those years' records); district-level counts for those years are slight undercounts. Neighborhood assignment is by point-in-polygon on reported coordinates. Census populations are protected by the Census Bureau's differential-privacy noise; small-area counts carry some uncertainty. One address generating many reports may reflect one persistent condition or one persistent caller.

    Reproducibility

    The fetch and aggregation scripts, with expected row counts asserted at every step, are in the project repository. Every number on this page is produced by scripts/aggregate.py from the raw downloads.
